🏭 Study Unit 7: Operations & Production Management

1. Transformation Model (Inputs → Process → Outputs)

  • Transformed Resources (worked on/converted): Materials (raw inputs), Information (data/schedules), Customers (haircuts/patients).
  • Transforming Resources (do the work): Facilities (buildings, machinery, equipment, technology) & Staff / Human Resources.
  • Goods vs Services: Goods = tangible, storable, low customer contact, capital-intensive. Services = intangible, simultaneous production & consumption, non-storable, high customer contact.

2. Process Types & Operations Layouts

  • Manufacturing Processes (Low → High Volume):
    • Project: One-off, complex, long duration (bridge/dam/ship).
    • Jobbing: Custom small orders (tailor-made suit, custom furniture).
    • Batch: Moderate volume in lots (bakery bread batches, clothing sizes).
    • Mass: High volume, standardized assembly line (cars, smartphones).
    • Continuous: 24/7 non-stop flow (Sasol petrochemical refinery, Eskom power).
  • Service Processes: Professional (high contact, custom: lawyers/doctors) → Service Shop (mixed: banks/hotels) → Mass Service (low contact, standardized: airline/supermarket).
  • 4 Layout Types:
    • Fixed-Position: Product stays stationary; workers/machines move around it (shipbuilding, construction, open-heart surgery).
    • Process (Functional): Similar machines/functions grouped together (hospitals, job shops).
    • Product (Line): Workstations in linear sequence matching assembly steps (car assembly, cafeteria line).
    • Cellular: Dissimilar machines grouped into a "cell" for a specific product family.

3. Inventory Formulas, JIT & Total Quality (TQM)

  • EOQ (Economic Order Quantity): EOQ = √[(2 × D × S) / H]
    (D = Annual Demand, S = Ordering cost per order, H = Holding/carrying cost per unit/year). Minimizes total ordering + holding costs.
  • Reorder Point (ROP): ROP = d × L (d = daily demand, L = lead time in days).
  • JIT (Just-in-Time): Pull system (triggered by actual customer demand via Kanban signals), zero/minimal buffer inventory, eliminates waste, requires ultra-reliable suppliers.
  • MRP (Material Requirements Planning): Push system using Master Production Schedule (MPS), Bill of Materials (BOM), and Inventory Records.
  • 4 TQM Costs of Quality:
    • Prevention Costs: Training, quality planning, supplier vetting (before production).
    • Appraisal Costs: Inspection, lab testing, audits during/after production.
    • Internal Failure: Scrap, rework, downtime caught before reaching customer.
    • External Failure: Warranty claims, product recalls, refunds, lost reputation after delivery to customer.

🎯 Study Unit 8: Marketing Management

1. Orientations, Consumer Behaviour & Research

  • Marketing Orientations:
    • Production: Focus on internal manufacturing efficiency & low cost.
    • Sales: Aggressive promotion/pushing of unsought or excess stock.
    • Marketing (Consumer): Customer satisfaction + long-term profitability + organizational integration.
    • Societal Marketing: Balances customer wants + company profits + long-term societal/environmental welfare.
  • 4 Core Pillars of the Marketing Concept: (1) Consumer orientation, (2) Long-term profit orientation, (3) Organisational integration, (4) Social responsibility.
  • Consumer Decision Process (5 Steps):
    1. Need/Problem Recognition → 2. Information Search → 3. Evaluation of Alternatives → 4. Purchase Decision → 5. Post-Purchase Evaluation (Cognitive Dissonance = buyer's remorse/doubt after major purchase).
  • Research Data: Secondary data (already exists: Stats SA, past sales reports — cheaper/faster) vs Primary data (newly collected for current problem: surveys, focus groups, observation).

2. STP (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning)

  • 4 Bases of Market Segmentation:
    • Geographic: Province, city, urban/rural, climate.
    • Demographic: Age, gender, income, education, family size, LSM.
    • Psychographic: Lifestyle, personality, social class, values.
    • Behavioural: Usage rate (heavy/light users), brand loyalty, benefits sought, occasion.
  • 3 Target Marketing Strategies:
    • Undifferentiated (Mass): 1 marketing mix for the entire market (ignores segment differences).
    • Differentiated (Multi-segment): Separate tailored marketing mixes for 2+ distinct segments (e.g. Toyota Corolla vs Hilux vs Lexus).
    • Concentrated (Niche): Focuses all resources on 1 specific niche segment.
  • Positioning: Creating a distinct image/perception of the brand in the minds of target customers relative to competitors.

3. The 4 Ps & Product Life Cycle (PLC)

  • Product (Consumer Goods Classification):
    • Convenience: Frequent, low price, minimal effort (bread, milk, toothpaste).
    • Shopping: Compared on price/quality/style across stores (furniture, appliances, clothes).
    • Specialty: Unique brand appeal; buyers won't accept substitutes (Rolex, Ferrari).
  • Price Strategies for New Products:
    • Price Skimming: Launch at a high price to recover R&D from early adopters, then lower gradually (tech/smartphones).
    • Market Penetration Pricing: Launch at a low price to rapidly capture massive market share and deter competitors.
  • Place (Market Coverage Intensity):
    • Intensive: Every possible outlet (Coca-Cola, Simba chips).
    • Selective: Handpicked retailers (high-end electronics, designer clothes).
    • Exclusive: 1 authorized dealer per region (luxury cars).
  • Product Life Cycle (4 Stages):
    1. Introduction: Slow sales, high unit cost, zero/negative profit, build awareness.
    2. Growth: Rapidly rising sales, competitors enter, profits rise.
    3. Maturity: Sales peak & plateau, intense price competition, profits level off/decline.
    4. Decline: Falling sales & profits (harvest or divest).

💼 Study Unit 9: Financial Management, HR & SA Labour Legislation

1. Financial Principles, Ratios & Break-Even

  • Primary Goal: Long-term maximisation of owner/shareholder wealth (value of the firm).
  • Liquidity vs Solvency:
    • Liquidity: Ability to pay short-term debts as they fall due (<12 months).
    • Solvency: Total Assets exceed Total Liabilities (long-term survival).
  • 3 Core Principles: (1) Cost-Benefit (marginal benefits > marginal costs), (2) Risk-Return (higher risk demands higher expected return), (3) Time Value of Money (R1 today is worth more than R1 in the future due to interest/earning potential).
  • Must-Know Formulas:
    • Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities (Norm: 2:1)
    • Quick / Acid-Test Ratio = (Current Assets − Inventory) ÷ Current Liabilities (Norm: 1:1 — excludes inventory because it is least liquid!)
    • Net Working Capital (NWC) = Current Assets − Current Liabilities
    • Debt Ratio = (Total Debt ÷ Total Assets) × 100
    • Break-Even Quantity (QBE) = Total Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price/unit − Variable Cost/unit)

2. Human Resource Management (HRM)

  • Job Analysis = Job Description vs Job Specification:
    • Job Description: Focuses on the JOB (duties, tasks, responsibilities, reporting line, working conditions).
    • Job Specification: Focuses on the PERSON (minimum qualifications, degree, years of experience, skills required).
  • Recruitment vs Selection:
    • Recruitment: Attracting a pool of qualified applicants (Internal = promotions/transfers; raises morale & cheaper, vs External = job portals/universities; brings fresh ideas/innovation).
    • Selection: Choosing the best candidate from the pool (CV screening → psychometric tests → interviews → reference checks → medical/offer).
  • Training vs Development: Training = technical skills for current job (short-term). Development = broad managerial growth for future roles (long-term).
  • Appraisal Biases: Halo effect (1 good trait inflates all ratings), Central tendency (rating everyone average/middle), Recency (only remembering recent weeks).

3. South African Labour Legislation (High-Yield!)

  • BCEA (Basic Conditions of Employment Act):
    • Max ordinary hours: 45 hours/week (9h/day for ≤5-day week; 8h/day for >5-day week).
    • Max overtime: 10 hours/week paid at 1.5× normal wage (or 2.0× on Sundays & Public Holidays).
    • Annual leave: 21 consecutive days paid (15 working days) per year.
    • Sick leave: 30 days (5-day worker) or 36 days (6-day worker) per 36-month cycle.
    • Maternity leave: 4 consecutive months; Family responsibility: 3 days/year.
  • LRA (Labour Relations Act):
    • 3 Fair Dismissal Grounds ONLY: (1) Misconduct (theft/dishonesty), (2) Incapacity (poor performance or ill health), (3) Operational Requirements (retrenchment).
    • Requires BOTH Substantive Fairness (valid fair reason) AND Procedural Fairness (fair hearing/process).
    • Unfair dismissal disputes referred to CCMA within 30 days (First step: Conciliation → then Arbitration).
  • EEA (Employment Equity Act): Eliminates unfair discrimination & implements Affirmative Action for designated groups (Black people [Africans, Coloureds, Indians], women, people with disabilities).
  • SDA & SDL (Skills Development): 1% Skills Development Levy on payroll funds SETAs & National Skills Fund.
